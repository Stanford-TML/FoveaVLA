A wrist camera gives a robot a close-up, but one that is tied to its hand. What if the policy could decide where to look?

We present FoveaVLA, a vision-language-action model that replaces the wrist camera with a learned foveation action. Alongside every action chunk, it predicts the interaction region it will need to see at the next policy query, then crops that region from the original-resolution external image. The policy thus receives both the full view for scene context and the crop for close-up detail, without a camera on the hand.

03 · Same pixel budget

Why crop? Resolution where the interaction happens

The vision encoder sees 224×224 pixels as a 16×16 grid of patch tokens. When a 1280×720 frame is resized, the interaction region covers only a few of these tokens; a crop of the same region from the original image covers all 256.

04 · Method

Learn where to look, alongside how to act

FoveaVLA builds on π0.5. From one shared observation it produces two outputs: an action chunk from the flow-matching action expert, and a foveation action, one box per external view, decoded with PaliGemma's existing location tokens.

Supervision from existing labels

Interaction boxes come from RoboInter annotations on DROID: the smallest rectangle enclosing the components each annotated skill requires, such as the gripper, the manipulated object, and the target region.

Predicts the next region

The target is the interaction region at the next query, because the gripper and objects move while the chunk executes.

No added wait

Box tokens are decoded from the cached prefix while the robot executes the chunk, so the crop is ready when the next frame arrives.

05 · Results

Real-world evaluation

Four real-world suites on the DROID setup, 12 shared randomized trials each. Both FoveaVLA variants match the wrist-equipped baseline on plain pick-and-place and achieve higher success with distractors, cloth manipulation, and far-away objects.

π0.5 (wrist-equipped) FoveaVLA-Single FoveaVLA-Dual

What makes it work

With one external camera and no wrist view, removing either the foveal crop or depth lowers success substantially. With both, FoveaVLA-Single reaches 70.8%, above the wrist-equipped π0.5.

Where the policy attends

On a Move episode, π0.5 attends almost exclusively to the wrist view, so it can miss a target outside it. FoveaVLA keeps a steady share on the full external views, which cover the workspace, and draws close-up detail from the foveal crops.
